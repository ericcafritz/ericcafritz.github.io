Abstract

This essay is an extension of method models described in The Science of Methods by Eric Cafritz, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18733134 (referred to below as the "Register"), and in particular in the companion paper A Sheaf-Theoretic Model of Methods, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20312085 (the "Sheaf-Theoretic Model"). The essay is intended to be readable on a standalone basis.  Parts I and II of the Register supply the foundations and grammar for the execution model in this essay. To the extent required, defined terms from the Register and the Sheaf-Theoretic Model are recapitulated below. Much of the Register, as well as the Sheaf-Theoretic Model, concerns the retrospective examination of established or crystallized methods, whose configurations have been fixed by design or practice. This essay describes methods in application in formal terms. It presents executions as sampled runs, from which patterns in a method can be read, together with the records of completed applications. This execution model investigates which characteristics carry or preserve method identity, so that a method remains distinctively recognizable despite variation among the steps taken in its applications. The essay also focuses on the limits of formalization, examining the points at which an agent's determination is required in application. At each such point, the ensuing formal consequences depend upon the determination supplied by the agent.
